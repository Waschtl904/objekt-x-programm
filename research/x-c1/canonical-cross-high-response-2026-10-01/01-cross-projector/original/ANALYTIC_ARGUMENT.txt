Prepared analytic argument for the post-integration experiment
===========================================================

Scope. Q is the already positive physical operator at A9 or A11. P is its
canonical critical spectral projector. Existing certificates place its
spectrum in (0,theta] union [nu,infinity), where theta < nu. The original
trial columns U are exactly L2 orthonormal by their defined Cholesky
normalization. All uses of positivity and spectral separation are
retrospective; they do not supply a forward Renewal theorem.

0. Cheap common projector geometry before shifted solves

Define VA=PA UA and EA=VA-UA GA, with GA=UA*PA UA. Then UA*EA=0 and
EA*EA=GA-GA^2. The same holds at B. With O=UA*J*UB,

  K=GA O GB + GA UA*J*EB + EA*J*UB GB + EA*J*EB.

For entry i,j the three correction radii are bounded by

  sqrt((GA-GA^2)_ii [GB(I-O*O)GB]_jj),
  sqrt((GB-GB^2)_jj [GA(I-OO*)GA]_ii),
  sqrt((GA-GA^2)_ii (GB-GB^2)_jj).

Every product uses the same projected Gram matrices. In the diagonal
covariances one may also intersect with G_ii and (I-G)_ii, since
0<=G<=I. These identities do not provide separately signed actual high
cross products, but they retain known signed portions of their sum.

A second, forward estimate gives a useful intersection. Since

  K_ij=(GA O)_ij + <J EA_i,UB_j>
       - <(I-PB)J VA_i,(I-PB)UB_j>,

its radius around (GA O)_ij is at most

  sqrt((GA-GA^2)_ii (I-O*O)_jj)
       + sqrt(SA_ii/nu_B * (I-GB)_jj).

The second summand uses forward formnaturality qB(JVA)=qA(VA), the actual
B-complement floor nu_B, and qA(PA UA_i)<=SA_ii. No backward form identity
for restriction and no inverse intertwining is used. Adding the energy
radius sqrt(SA_ii SB_jj)/17 converts each K bound to a Y bound.

1. Coordinate residuals

The full Mellin coordinate map M satisfies G=M* M=I+t t* >= I. Split its
raw coordinates into the published finite low block and the COMPLETE high
space. The operator form has blocks [L B; B* H]. Published data give
||L-L0|| <= eL, B B* <= Hup, and ||t_high|| <= epsilon_t.

For a trial column u=M[v;0] and a fixed complex low candidate w=M[x;0],
the residual of (Q-z)w=u, pulled back by M*, has the exact two components

  r_low  = G_low v - (L-z G_low)x,
  r_high = t_high t_low* (v+z x) - B* x.

Thus

  ||r_low|| <= ||G_low v-(L0-z G_low)x|| + eL ||x|| = rho_low,
  ||r_high|| <= sqrt(x* Hup x)
                 + epsilon_t |t_low*(v+z x)| = rho_high.

Since ||M^{-*}|| <= 1, the PHYSICAL residual norm is at most
sqrt(rho_low^2+rho_high^2). This includes the entire high response, not a
finite truncation of it. For complex x, x*Hup x equals the sum of the real
and imaginary quadratic forms because Hup is real symmetric.

For a certified d_z <= dist(z,spectrum(Q)), the spectral theorem gives

  ||(Q-z)^-1 u - w|| <= sqrt(rho_low^2+rho_high^2)/d_z.

An approximate solution need not be an interval-certified exact solve:
the computed dyadic midpoint x is an exact candidate, and the full
residual above certifies it independently of the solver's accuracy.

2. Rational filter

Choose t=1/300 and m=8, with theta<t<nu checked from the source bounds.
For f(lambda)=1/(1+(lambda/t)^m),

  ||P-f(Q)|| <= delta,
  delta=max((theta/t)^m/(1+(theta/t)^m),
            1/(1+(nu/t)^m)).

The simple poles are z_k=t exp(i(2k+1)pi/m). Since z_k^m=-t^m, the
residue of f at z_k is -z_k/m. Therefore

  f(Q)=sum_k (-z_k/m)(Q-z_k)^-1.

Real inputs allow four conjugate pairs to be evaluated as twice the real
part. Their poles lie away from both certified spectral intervals. Their
real parts can be positive; the proof uses their full complex distance to
the spectrum, not an unjustified positive-real-shift coercivity bound.

Let Vtilde be the sum of the low candidates with these same coefficients.
For each original unit-norm trial column,

  ||P u - Vtilde|| <= delta
       + sum_upper_poles (2 |z_k|/m)
          sqrt(rho_low,k^2+rho_high,k^2)/d_zk = eta.

All poles, residuals, norms, distances and polynomial overlaps will be
evaluated with outward interval arithmetic. Candidate midpoints are fixed
dyadic values; input interval uncertainty remains in every residual.

3. Joint signed cross terms

Write RAtilde=UA-VAtilde and RBtilde=UB-VBtilde. Directed polynomial
integration computes their joint products with the same physical embedding
J. In particular, with the same approximate columns throughout,

  Ktilde=O-RAtilde*J*UB-UA*J*RBtilde+RAtilde*J*RBtilde.

For entries i,j, the two mixed residual radii are eta_A,i and eta_B,j.
The cross-residual radius is bounded by

  eta_A,i ||RBtilde_j|| + eta_B,j ||RAtilde_i||
       + eta_A,i eta_B,j.

The sharper direct K radius is the minimum of

  eta_A,i ||VBtilde_j|| + eta_B,j,
  eta_A,i + eta_B,j ||VAtilde_i||,

using ||PA UA_i||, ||PB UB_j|| <= 1. These follow from two exact product
decompositions; no independence of the three cross terms is assumed.

The normalization remains BB=GB+LB/17 and Y=K GB^-1 BB. Equivalently,

  Y_ij=K_ij+q_B(J PA UA_i,PB UB_j)/17.

Formnaturality, spectral energy monotonicity and Cauchy-Schwarz for the
already positive forms give

  |Y_ij-K_ij| <= sqrt(SA_ii SB_jj)/17,

where SA=UA*QA UA and SB=UB*QB UB are the published trial energy matrices.
This preserves the factor 17 and uses the physical inverse-response
maximizer convention of the preceding certificates.

4. Acceptance boundaries

These formulas provide actual-operator enclosures, conditional on the
published operator, Gram, spectral and transport certificates. They do not
guarantee a useful width. A very accurate scalar filter may coexist with a
large complete-solve residual, particularly the sqrt(x*Hup x) term.

Only a certified angle corridor under 10 degrees total width together with
exclusion of a previous counterfamily qualifies as GREEN. Wide correct
enclosures qualify as UNRESOLVED, not STRUCTURAL OPEN 2. The latter requires
new explicitly feasible jointly coupled completions satisfying the new
data. No numerical direction or surviving interval box is substituted for
these obligations.
