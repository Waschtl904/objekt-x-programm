## 1. Defect in the original consecutive-convergent return count

The original UC proof used consecutive convergents

\[
\frac{k_j}{n_j},\qquad \frac{k_{j+1}}{n_{j+1}}
\]

of `alpha/ell` and wrote both return matrices as

\[
G_j=A^{n_j-k_j}B^{k_j}.
\]

That formula is correct only when

\[
\varepsilon_j:=n_j\alpha-k_j\ell>0.
\]

For a convergent on the opposite side,

\[
\varepsilon_j<0,
\]

one has

\[
\left\lfloor\frac{n_j\alpha}{\ell}\right\rfloor=k_j-1,
\]

so the actual numbers of wrap and no-wrap steps are

\[
(k_j-1,\ n_j-k_j+1),
\]

not `(k_j,n_j-k_j)`.

Since consecutive convergents alternate sides, the claimed unimodular return-exponent basis in the original proof is not justified by the displayed vectors.

The old regression repeated the same convention and therefore did not detect the defect.

Status:

\[
\boxed{\text{original consecutive-convergent return calculation (UC.5)}\quad\times[M].}
\]

This is a proof-step failure, not a counterexample to the boundary theorem.

## 2. Repaired one-sided near-return UC lemma

### Lemma A14.3a-UC2

Let `ell>0`, `0<alpha<ell`, and

\[
\frac{\alpha}{\ell}\notin\mathbb Q.
\]

Let

\[
R_\alpha(t)=t+\alpha\pmod\ell
\]

be the irrational rotation of the circle `C_ell`.

Let

\[
A,B\in GL_2(\mathbb C),\qquad AB=BA,
\]

and let a measurable vector field

\[
X:C_\ell\to\mathbb C^2
\]

satisfy a.e.

\[
X(R_\alpha t)=AX(t),\qquad 0<t<\ell-\alpha,
\tag{UC2.1}
\]

and

\[
X(R_\alpha t)=BX(t),\qquad \ell-\alpha<t<\ell.
\tag{UC2.2}
\]

Put

\[
V:=\operatorname{span}\binom01.
\]

Assume:

1. the first component of `X` vanishes a.e. on a nonempty open interval `G` of the circle;
2. no positive power of `A` preserves `V`:
   \[
   A^N V\ne V\qquad(N\ge1).
   \tag{UC2.3}
   \]

Then

\[
\boxed{X=0\quad\text{a.e. on }C_\ell.}
\tag{UC2.4}
\]

### Proof

Choose an ordinary coordinate subinterval

\[
J=(u,v)\Subset G\cap(0,\ell).
\]

Because `alpha/ell` is irrational, the positive fractional returns are dense.  Hence there are integers

\[
n_j\to\infty,
\qquad
k_j:=\left\lfloor\frac{n_j\alpha}{\ell}\right\rfloor,
\]

such that

\[
\theta_j:=n_j\alpha-k_j\ell>0,
\qquad
\theta_j\to0.
\tag{UC2.5}
\]

For all sufficiently large `j`,

\[
I_j:=(u,v-\theta_j)
\]

has positive length and satisfies

\[
R_\alpha^{n_j}t=t+\theta_j\in J
\qquad(t\in I_j).
\tag{UC2.6}
\]

Because `t+theta_j<ell` on this chosen lift, the total number of wraps in these `n_j` steps is **exactly** `k_j`; the number of no-wrap steps is `n_j-k_j`.  Commutativity therefore gives the genuine typed return matrix

\[
G_j=A^{n_j-k_j}B^{k_j}.
\tag{UC2.7}
\]

Define the actual exponent vectors

\[
v_j:=(n_j-k_j,k_j)\in\mathbb Z^2.
\tag{UC2.8}
\]

Since

\[
\frac{k_j}{n_j}\to\frac\alpha\ell,
\]

one has

\[
\frac{k_j}{n_j-k_j}
\longrightarrow
\frac{\alpha}{\ell-\alpha},
\tag{UC2.9}
\]

and the limit is irrational.

Suppose first that for some sufficiently large `j`,

\[
G_jV\ne V.
\]

For a.e. `t in I_j`, both `t` and `R_alpha^{n_j}t` lie in `G`, so

\[
X(t)\in V,
\qquad
G_jX(t)=X(R_\alpha^{n_j}t)\in V.
\]

Since `G_j` is invertible and `G_jV` is a one-dimensional line distinct from `V`,

\[
V\cap G_j^{-1}V=\{0\}.
\]

Hence

\[
X=0\quad\text{a.e. on }I_j.
\tag{UC2.10}
\]

The forward iterates of a nonempty interval under an irrational rotation cover the circle.  The one-step cocycle therefore propagates zero to all of `C_ell`, modulo a countable union of null exceptional sets.

It remains to exclude the alternative that

\[
G_jV=V
\]

for every sufficiently large positive near-return.

Let `H` be the subgroup of `Z^2` generated by all differences

\[
v_i-v_j.
\]

Because every `G_j` preserves `V`, every

\[
A^mB^n\qquad((m,n)\in H)
\]

preserves `V`.

The subgroup `H` has rank two.  Indeed, if it had rank at most one, all sufficiently large `v_j` would lie in an affine rank-one lattice

\[
v_0+\mathbb Z w,
\qquad w\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}.
\]

Since the vectors are unbounded, their coordinate ratio would then tend to the rational slope of `w`.  This contradicts the irrational limit in `(UC2.9)`.

Thus `H` is a rank-two subgroup of `Z^2`, hence has finite index.  Consequently there exists `N>=1` with

\[
(N,0)\in H.
\]

Therefore

\[
A^NV=V,
\]

contrary to `(UC2.3)`.

So some positive near-return necessarily satisfies `G_jV != V`, and the first part of the proof applies.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{X=0\quad\text{a.e. on }C_\ell.}
\]

`\square`

Status:

\[
\boxed{\text{one-sided typed irrational-rotation UC lemma A14.3a-UC2}\quad\checkmark[M].}
\]

The proof uses only actual piecewise cocycle branches and actual wrap counts.  No untyped affine composition and no sign-indefinite convergent bookkeeping is used.

## 3. Arithmetic input: `log 3 / log 2` is transcendental

Set

\[
\rho:=\frac{\log3}{\log2}.
\]

First `rho` is irrational: if `rho=m/n in Q`, then

\[
3^n=2^m,
\]

impossible by unique factorization.

If `rho` were algebraic irrational, the Gelfond-Schneider theorem applied to the algebraic base `2` would imply that

\[
2^\rho
\]

is transcendental.  But

\[
2^\rho=3.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\rho\text{ is transcendental}.}
\tag{R.1}
\]

For the coefficient estimates put

\[
\beta:=\frac qp=2^{-3/4},
\qquad
 t:=\frac rp.
\]

Then

\[
\frac12<\beta<1.
\tag{R.2}
\]

Moreover

\[
t^2
=\rho\left(\frac23\right)^{3/2}.
\tag{R.3}
\]

The elementary prime-power comparisons

\[
3^2>2^3,
\qquad
3^3<2^5
\]

give

\[
\frac32<\rho<\frac53.
\]

Also

\[
\left(\frac23\right)^{3/2}>\frac49,
\]

and

\[
\left(\frac23\right)^{3/2}<\frac35
\]

(the second follows after squaring from `8/27<9/25`).  Therefore

\[
\boxed{\frac23<t^2<1.}
\tag{R.4}
\]

In particular

\[
\sqrt{\frac23}<t<1.
\]

## 4. Lower circle: the no-wrap matrix has no finite invariant-line power

Keep the valid lower cocycle from the original A14.3a audit:

\[
\ell=d,
\qquad
\alpha=e,
\qquad
A=E_0,
\qquad
B=M_\delta^{-1},
\]

where

\[
E_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
(p^2-r^2)/(qr)&-p/r\\[1mm]
p/r&-q/r
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{R.5}
\]

The identities

\[
\det E_0=1,
\qquad
E_0M_\delta=M_\delta E_0
\]

remain unchanged.

A direct calculation gives

\[
(\operatorname{tr}E_0)^2-4
=
\frac{(p-q-r)(p-q+r)(p+q-r)(p+q+r)}{q^2r^2}.
\tag{R.6}
\]

The three positive numbers `p,q,r` satisfy the strict triangle inequalities.  Indeed, by `(R.2)` and `(R.4)`,

\[
\frac qp>\frac12,
\qquad
\frac rp>\sqrt{\frac23}>\frac12,
\qquad
\frac rp<1.
\]

Thus

\[
p<q+r,
\qquad q<p+r,
\qquad r<p+q.
\]

Hence the right side of `(R.6)` is negative:

\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{tr}E_0|<2.}
\tag{R.7}
\]

So `E_0 in SL_2(R)` is elliptic.

Its trace is

\[
\operatorname{tr}E_0
=
\frac{1-\beta^2-t^2}{\beta t}.
\tag{R.8}
\]

If this trace were algebraic, then `t` would satisfy

\[
t^2+\beta(\operatorname{tr}E_0)t+\beta^2-1=0
\]

with algebraic coefficients.  Thus `t` would be algebraic, and `(R.3)` would force `rho` algebraic, contradicting `(R.1)`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{tr}E_0\text{ is transcendental}.}
\tag{R.9}
\]

A finite-order real `SL_2` matrix has roots of unity as eigenvalues, hence algebraic trace.  Thus `E_0` is not of finite order.

Because `E_0` is elliptic, if a positive power `E_0^N` preserved a real line, it would have to be `+I` or `-I`; that would make `E_0` finite order.  Hence

\[
\boxed{E_0^NV\ne V\quad(N\ge1).}
\tag{R.10}
\]

All hypotheses of A14.3a-UC2 now hold for the lower circle.  Therefore

\[
w=0\quad\text{a.e. on }(0,d).
\]

Exactly as in the original audit, the two components of `w(x)=(g(x),g(a-x))^T` then cover `(0,a)` and give

\[
\boxed{h=0\quad\text{a.e. on }(0,a).}
\tag{R.11}
\]

Status:

\[
\boxed{\text{lower-half UC, repaired}\quad\checkmark[M].}
\]

## 5. Upper circle: the no-wrap matrix has no finite invariant-line power

After `(R.11)`, retain the valid upper reduction from the original audit:

\[
F(z):
ql(z)+pl(a-z)+rl(e+z)+rl(e-z)=0,
\qquad 0<z<a,
\]

with the genuine left support gap

\[
l(z)=0
\qquad(0<z<2a-S).
\]

On the upper circle `(0,e)` the valid cocycle is

\[
\ell=e,
\qquad
\alpha=\delta,
\qquad
A=A_U,
\qquad
B=N_\eta^{-1}.
\]

Write

\[
\gamma:=\frac{p^2-q^2}{pr}
=\frac{1-\beta^2}{t}>0.
\]

Then the no-wrap matrix is

\[
A_U=
\begin{pmatrix}
\beta&-\gamma\\[1mm]
\gamma&(1-\gamma^2)/\beta
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det A_U=1.
\tag{R.12}
\]

The original algebraic identity

\[
A_UN_\eta=N_\eta A_U
\]

remains unchanged.

By `(R.2)` and `(R.4)`,

\[
\gamma=(1-\beta)\frac{1+\beta}{t}.
\]

Since `t<1<1+beta`,

\[
\gamma>1-\beta.
\]

Since `t>1/2>1-beta`,

\[
\gamma<1+\beta.
\]

Therefore

\[
|\beta-\gamma|<1<\beta+\gamma.
\tag{R.13}
\]

A direct calculation gives

\[
(\operatorname{tr}A_U)^2-4
=
\frac{(\beta-\gamma-1)(\beta-\gamma+1)
(\beta+\gamma-1)(\beta+\gamma+1)}{\beta^2}<0.
\tag{R.14}
\]

Thus `A_U` is elliptic.

Also

\[
\operatorname{tr}A_U
=
\beta+\frac1\beta-\frac{\gamma^2}{\beta}.
\tag{R.15}
\]

If this trace were algebraic, then

\[
\gamma^2=1+\beta^2-\beta\operatorname{tr}A_U
\]

would be algebraic.  Since

\[
\gamma^2=\frac{(1-\beta^2)^2}{t^2}
\]

and `1-beta^2 != 0`, this would force `t^2`, hence `rho`, algebraic, again contradicting `(R.1)`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{tr}A_U\text{ is transcendental}.}
\tag{R.16}
\]

So `A_U` is elliptic and not of finite order, and therefore

\[
\boxed{A_U^NV\ne V\quad(N\ge1).}
\tag{R.17}
\]

A14.3a-UC2 applies to the upper circle.  Hence

\[
W=0\quad\text{a.e. on }(0,e).
\]

The already verified low/high relations then propagate this to all of `(0,a)`, so

\[
\boxed{h=0\quad\text{a.e. on }(a,2a).}
\tag{R.18}
\]

Status:

\[
\boxed{\text{upper-half UC, repaired}\quad\checkmark[M].}
\]
