# P11 End-to-End Referee R36-A14.2a — local incidence group and global-group firewall

Date: 2026-08-20

## Purpose

A14.1 reduces `Lh=0` to finitely many visibility cells, but it does not yet identify a global
finite scalar system.  This note makes the first exact A14.2 incidence statement.

The key distinction is between:

1. the affine chart group generated **inside a full four-branch cell**;
2. the domain-typed incidence pseudogroup generated by **all** visibility cells.

Only the first is completely identified here.  A concrete middle-`u` cell shows that the second
is strictly richer than the first, so no global `Dih(d Z)` claim is booked.

No two-shift kernel classification, R36-A, or R30-F conclusion is made.

---

## 1. Setup in the first genuine two-shift chamber

Write

\[
\tau_2=\frac{\log2}{2},\qquad
\tau_3=\frac{\log3}{2},\qquad
 d:=\tau_3-\tau_2=\frac12\log\frac32.
\]

Assume

\[
\tau_3\le T_0<\log2.
\]

On a full four-branch visibility cell `C`, A14.1c' implies `0<u<tau_2`.  Introduce the chart
parameter

\[
s:=\tau_2+u.
\]

The four positive arguments in the same cell equation are then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi_0(s)&=s,\\
\chi_1(s)&=2\tau_2-s,\\
\chi_2(s)&=s+d,\\
\chi_3(s)&=2\tau_2+d-s.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A14.2a.1}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
r(s):=2\tau_2-s,
\qquad
t(s):=s+d,
\]

one has

\[
\chi_1=r,\qquad \chi_2=t,\qquad \chi_3=t\circ r.
\]

## 2. Proposition R36-A14.2a — full-four-branch chart group

The affine group generated by the chart transitions of a full four-branch cell is

\[
\boxed{
G_4=\langle r,t\mid r^2=1,\ rtr=t^{-1}\rangle
=\{s\mapsto s+nd,\ s\mapsto 2\tau_2-s+nd:n\in\mathbb Z\}.
}
\tag{A14.2a.2}
\]

Thus `G_4` is the infinite dihedral group over the translation lattice `d Z`.

### Proof

The four charts (A14.2a.1) already contain the generators `r` and `t`.  Since

\[
r^2=\mathrm{id},
\qquad
rtr(s)=s-d=t^{-1}(s),
\]

the generated group is dihedral.  Every reduced word is either `t^n` or `t^n r`, hence has one
of the two displayed affine forms.  Conversely each displayed map is generated by `r,t`.
`\square`

Status:

\[
\boxed{\text{R36-A14.2a (full-four-branch chart group)}\quad\checkmark[M].}
\]

### Corollary R36-A14.2a.1 — local `<=8` orbit bound

Let an orbit of `G_4` be intersected with the positive half-annulus `(R,S)`.  It is contained in

\[
\{s+nd:n\in\mathbb Z\}
\cup
\{2\tau_2-s+nd:n\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]

In the two-shift chamber

\[
S-R<S<T_0<\log2.
\]

Moreover

\[
4d=2\log\frac32=\log\frac94>\log2.
\]

Hence an interval of length `<log2` contains at most four points from any arithmetic progression
of spacing `d`.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
|G_4\cdot s\cap(R,S)|\le8.
}
\tag{A14.2a.3}
\]

Status:

\[
\boxed{\text{local full-four-branch orbit bound }\le8\quad\checkmark[M].}
\]

This is a bound for the `G_4` chart closure only.  It is **not** a global A14.2 orbit bound.

## 3. Counterexample to the global `G_4` identification

Other visibility cells generate affine transitions not contained in `G_4`.

Take, for example, a chamber-2 configuration with

\[
R=0.05,\qquad S=0.50,\qquad T_0=0.56,
\]

and choose `u` with

\[
\tau_2<u<\tau_3,
\]

for instance `u=0.45`.  Then

\[
x:=u-\tau_2\approx0.1034264\in(R,S),
\]

and

\[
y:=\tau_3-u\approx0.0993061\in(R,S).
\]

The forward branches are outside `(R,S)`:

\[
u+\tau_2>S,
\qquad
u+\tau_3>S.
\]

Thus the cell equation contains the two folded arguments `x,y`, with the affine incidence
relation

\[
\boxed{y=d-x.}
\tag{A14.2a.4}
\]

This is the reflection

\[
q(s):=d-s
\]

about `d/2`.

But the reflections in `G_4` have the form

\[
s\mapsto2\tau_2-s+nd,
\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

If `q` belonged to `G_4`, one would have

\[
d=2\tau_2+nd
\]

for some integer `n`, hence

\[
2\tau_2=(1-n)d.
\]

Using `2\tau_2=\log2` and `d=\tfrac12\log(3/2)`, this would give

\[
2\log2=(1-n)\log\frac32.
\]

Exponentiating yields an equality of a power of `2` with a power involving `3`, impossible by
unique prime factorisation.  Hence

\[
\boxed{q\notin G_4.}
\tag{A14.2a.5}
\]

Therefore the statement

> the global two-shift incidence group is `Dih(d Z)`

is false as stated.

Status:

\[
\boxed{\text{global incidence group }=G_4\quad\times[M].}
\]

The error is domain-typing: the full-four-branch cell sees one family of chart transitions, while
middle-`u` cells can realise different reflections.  This is precisely why A14.2 must be a
**domain-typed incidence pseudogroup/graph**, not an untyped abstract orbit group.

## 4. Consequence for A14.2 architecture

The correct next object has:

- vertices: branch charts together with their source/image intervals;
- edges: co-occurrence transitions that are actually realised on a visibility cell;
- labels: affine maps and weight/sign ratios;
- propagation status per connected domain component: `killed`, `weighted-cycle killed`, or
  `unresolved`.

A scalar matrix is permitted only after a connected component has been reduced to finitely many
compatible affine charts.

The local weighted-cycle lemma from the A14.1 hardening addendum may then be applied whenever a
closed domain-preserving cycle has total multiplier different from `1`.

Status:

\[
\boxed{\text{R36-A14.2 global domain-typed incidence closure}\quad?[O].}
\]

The earlier regression point `(R,S,T_0)=(0.10,0.50,0.56)` remains a regression target only.  No
`ker L={0}` theorem for that point is promoted here.
