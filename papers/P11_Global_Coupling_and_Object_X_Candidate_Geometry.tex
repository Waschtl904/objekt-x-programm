\begin{abstract}
P11 develops a finite-horizon Hilbert-space geometry for a source-first candidate of
Object~X and isolates, without overclaim, the remaining passage to a global terminal
geometry.  The construction couples a source-windowed archimedean Gamma form to a
source-dependent finite-adic martingale cutoff and a finite-horizon Feshbach--Schur
contribution.  The resulting graph spaces possess canonical bounded injective
transition maps and positive terminal metric operators; their normalized same-terminal
transitions are exact isometries.  The finite-window Gamma/Feshbach geometry is compact,
but its concrete Gamma-preconditioned Schur term lies in no finite Schatten class.  The
pure Gamma backbone has a canonical large-window Mosco/strong-resolvent limit, while the
finite-window compactness is lost in that limit.

The unnormalised terminal metrics themselves diverge on a dense smooth odd test core.
Their boundary growth is organized by a canonical infinite family of pullback-compatible
integral jets, while the normalized transport is governed exactly by a cross-terminal
Cauchy kernel.  On the odd sector, the future Schur term has a sharp fixed-vector
asymptotic of size $e^T/T^{2m+2}$; the corresponding mixed-jet sesquilinear form is also
asymptotically explicit for every fixed smooth odd pair.  The normalized fixed-pair
Schur geometry becomes asymptotically rank one, leaving uniform finite-jet Gram and
square-root control as a direct unresolved gate.

At fixed base terminal the odd condition number grows superpolynomially.  Consequently
one natural Jensen-product route to terminal convergence would require a square-root
compression defect that decays faster than every inverse power.  This diagnostic route
is kept logically separate from the direct cross-terminal problem.

The central unresolved source-level problem remains
\[
 W_{R,S,-}^{[T]}\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{\rm strong}?W_{R,S,-}^{[\infty]}.
\]
P11 proves neither this limit nor its failure, does not construct a final global
Object~X, and contains no proof of the Riemann hypothesis.  The paper separates proved
finite-horizon structure, route-specific diagnostics, and open global obligations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source-first finite-window geometry}\label{sec:source}

Fix $R>0$ and put
\[
 \mathscr H_R:=L^2(-R,R).
\]
Let $U_t$ denote translation on the ambient real line and write
\[
 D_s:=U_{s/2}-U_{-s/2}.
\]
For $0<R<S$, $E_{R,S}$ denotes zero extension from $(-R,R)$ to $(-S,S)$.  We write
\[
 E_R:\mathscr H_R\to L^2(\mathbb R)
\]
for zero extension to the real line and
\[
 \boxed{P_R:=E_R^*}
\]
for restriction to $(-R,R)$.

\subsection{Finite-adic martingale coordinates and source-dependent cutoff}

For each prime $p$, let $K_p^0$ be the centered $p$-adic mark sector with orthonormal
martingale basis
\[
 \{\psi_{p,j}:j\ge0\}.
\]
For $k\ge1$ define the normalized prime-power residual mark
\[
 \boxed{
 \eta_{p,k}
 =\sqrt{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}p^{(j-k)/2}\psi_{p,j}.
 }
\tag{2.1}\label{eq:eta-pk}
\]
Different prime sectors are orthogonal.  The source-dependent martingale depth is
\[
 \boxed{
 J_{p,R}(u)
 :=\max\left\{0,
 \left\lfloor\frac{2(R-|u|)_+}{\log p}\right\rfloor
 \right\}.
 }
\tag{2.2}\label{eq:martingale-depth}
\]
Define fiberwise
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathsf Q_R(u)\psi_{p,j}
 :=1_{\{j<J_{p,R}(u)\}}\psi_{p,j}.
 }
\tag{2.3}\label{eq:source-cutoff}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Source-coupled finite-adic conditioning]\label{prop:source-conditioning}
For every fixed $R$, $u\mapsto\mathsf Q_R(u)$ is a measurable field of orthogonal
projections.  Hence
\[
 (\mathsf Q_RF)(u):=\mathsf Q_R(u)F(u)
\]
defines a positive orthogonal projection on the corresponding finite-adic
$L^2$-field.  Moreover, with the convention that the sum is empty when
$J_{p,R}(u)=0$,
\[
 \mathsf Q_R(u)\eta_{p,k}
 =\sqrt{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k-1,J_{p,R}(u)-1)}
 p^{(j-k)/2}\psi_{p,j}.
\tag{2.4}\label{eq:projected-mark}
\]
In particular, the full $p^k$ mark is retained whenever
\[
 |u|+\frac{k}{2}\log p\le R,
\]
while deeper martingale levels are canonically removed near the source boundary.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:source-cutoff} is the orthogonal coordinate projection onto the first
$J_{p,R}(u)$ martingale levels.  The depth function is integer-valued and measurable,
so the resulting field is measurable.  Applying the projection to~\eqref{eq:eta-pk} gives
\eqref{eq:projected-mark}.  If $k\le J_{p,R}(u)$, all coordinates $0,\dots,k-1$ survive, which is
exactly the stated overlap condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the conditioning is genuinely source-first]\label{rem:sourcefirst}
The depth depends on $|u|$, so the resulting operator is not translation invariant.
This is precisely what is absent from a pure Haar or translation-invariant regulator.
The neutral finite-adic hub is fixed by every unital conditional expectation and must
therefore be treated separately by the Feshbach step below; the conditioning is not a
finished global Object-X compressor.
\end{remark}

The source-windowed neutral hub and conditioned residual operator are
\[
 \boxed{
 H_R
 =P_R\sum_{p^k\le e^{2R}}
 \sqrt{\log p}\,p^{-3k/4}D_{k\log p}E_R,
 }
\tag{2.5}\label{eq:hub-operator}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 (R_Rf)(u)
 =\sum_{p^k\le e^{2R}}
 \sqrt{\log p}\,p^{-k/4}
 D_{k\log p}E_Rf(u)\otimes\mathsf Q_R(u)\eta_{p,k}.
 }
\tag{2.6}\label{eq:rest-operator}
\]
For fixed $R$ these are bounded finite-window operators.

\subsection{Archimedean Gamma form}

The archimedean source form is encoded by
\[
 m_\Gamma(\xi):=1+g_\infty(\xi),
 \qquad
 m_\Gamma(\xi)\asymp\log(2+|\xi|).
\tag{2.7}\label{eq:gamma-symbol}
\]
Define
\[
 \mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[f,g]
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 m_\Gamma(\xi)\widehat{E_Rf}(\xi)
 \overline{\widehat{E_Rg}(\xi)}\,d\xi.
\tag{2.8}\label{eq:gamma-form}
\]
Let $C_{\Gamma,R}$ be the positive selfadjoint operator represented by this closed form.

\begin{proposition}[Gamma graph space]\label{prop:gamma}
For every fixed $R>0$, $\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}$ is densely defined, closed and positive,
$C_{\Gamma,R}\ge I$, and its form domain is a Hilbert space for the Gamma graph norm.
The embedding of this finite-window graph space into $L^2(-R,R)$ is compact; equivalently
$C_{\Gamma,R}^{-1}$ is compact.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Logarithmic, not Sobolev, regularity]\label{rem:log}
Up to equivalent fixed-window norms,
\[
 \mathcal D(\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R})
 \simeq
 \left\{f:\int\log(2+|\xi|)|\widehat{E_Rf}(\xi)|^2d\xi<\infty\right\}.
\]
It does not force the entire form domain into any fixed $H^s$, $s>0$.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite-horizon Feshbach geometry}\label{sec:feshbach}

Put
\[
 B_R:=(I+R_R^*R_R)^{-1},
\]
and define the positive source-level Schur contribution
\[
 \Sigma_R:=H_RB_RH_R^*\ge0.
\tag{3.1}\label{eq:schur-operator}
\]
The finite-level Object-X candidate form is
\[
 \boxed{
 q_R^X(f,g)
 :=\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[f,g]+\langle\Sigma_Rf,g\rangle.
 }
\tag{3.2}\label{eq:x-form}
\]
Because $H_R$ and $R_R$ are bounded at fixed $R$,
\[
 \mathcal D(q_R^X)=\mathcal D(\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Bounded Schur perturbation and graph norm]\label{prop:bounded-schur}
The operator
\[
 F_R^{\rm hub}:=C_{\Gamma,R}+\Sigma_R
\]
is positive selfadjoint with
\[
 \mathcal D(F_R^{\rm hub})=\mathcal D(C_{\Gamma,R}).
\]
Moreover
\[
 \mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[f]
 \le q_R^X(f)
 \le(1+\|H_R\|^2)\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[f].
\tag{3.3}\label{eq:graph-norm-equiv}
\]
Thus
\[
 \KXR{R}:=\bigl(\mathcal D(q_R^X),\|f\|_{X,R}^2:=q_R^X(f)\bigr)
\]
is a Hilbert space.  It is a finite-level graph space and is not identified here with a
final global Object~X.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $0<B_R\le I$, one has
\[
 0\le\langle\Sigma_Rf,f\rangle
 =\|B_R^{1/2}H_R^*f\|^2
 \le\|H_R\|^2\|f\|_2^2.
\]
As $\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[f]\ge\|f\|_2^2$, this gives~\eqref{eq:graph-norm-equiv}; closedness and the
operator-domain statement follow from the bounded perturbation theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Full-rest martingale representation}

The rest Gram is not an orthogonal direct sum over prime powers $k$ inside one prime
sector.  Its exact positive decomposition is indexed by prime/martingale-depth pairs
$(p,a)$.  To make the factorization self-contained, within this subsection write
\[
 K_s^{\rm tr}:=P_RD_sE_R
\]
for the source translation-difference operator.  This symbol is unrelated to the later
Gamma-preconditioned operator $K_R=C_{\Gamma,R}^{-1/2}H_R$.  Define
\[
 \Omega_{p,a,R}
 :=\{u\in[-R,R]:J_{p,R}(u)\ge a+1\};
\]
equivalently, up to endpoints,
\[
 \Omega_{p,a,R}
 =\left\{u:\ |u|\le R-\frac{a+1}{2}\log p\right\},
\]
with the set empty when the right-hand radius is negative.  Put
\[
 \Phi_{p,a,R}[f](u)
 :=\sum_{k\ge a+1}p^{-3k/4}(K_{k\log p}^{\rm tr}f)(u).
\]
At fixed $R$ the effectively active sums are finite.  Then
\[
 \mathfrak R_R(f,g)
 =\sum_p(\log p)(p-1)\sum_{a\ge0}p^a
 \int_{\Omega_{p,a,R}}
 \Phi_{p,a,R}[f](u)\overline{\Phi_{p,a,R}[g](u)}\,du.
\tag{3.4}\label{eq:rest-gram}
\]
Define the canonical analysis target
\[
 \mathscr Z_R:=\bigoplus_p\bigoplus_{a\ge0}L^2(\Omega_{p,a,R})
\]
and
\[
 (\widetilde R_Rf)_{p,a}(u)
 :=\sqrt{(\log p)(p-1)p^a}\,
 1_{\Omega_{p,a,R}}(u)\Phi_{p,a,R}[f](u).
\]
Polarization of~\eqref{eq:rest-gram} gives
\[
 \boxed{\widetilde R_R^*\widetilde R_R=R_R^*R_R.}
\tag{3.5}\label{eq:full-rest-gram}
\]
Thus
\[
 I+R_R^*R_R=I+\widetilde R_R^*\widetilde R_R,
\]
which is the exact full-rest denominator used below.

\begin{remark}[Primitive form-domination firewall]\label{rem:primitive}
We do not assume
\[
 R_R^*R_R\stackrel?\ge(R_R^{(1)})^*R_R^{(1)}.
\]
Different prime powers in one prime sector are nonorthogonal.  The future-certificate
transfer therefore uses the full martingale analysis operator
$\widetilde R_R$ and the $a=0$ primitive-plus-tail decomposition, rather than this
unproved shortcut.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Finite-window Schatten profile}

Define the Gamma-preconditioned hub operator and normalized Schur term
\[
 K_R:=C_{\Gamma,R}^{-1/2}H_R,
 \qquad
 S_R:=K_RB_RK_R^*.
\tag{3.6}\label{eq:gamma-preconditioned-schur}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Compact but no finite Schatten order]\label{thm:schatten}
For every nontrivial source level
\[
 R>\frac12\log2,
\]
one has
\[
 \boxed{S_R\in\cK,}
\]
but
\[
 \boxed{S_R\notin\Sp_p\qquad\text{for every finite }1\le p<\infty.}
\tag{3.7}\label{eq:schatten-nogo}
\]
In particular, the concrete source-windowed Gamma preconditioning plus C1z-B
Feshbach geometry does not supply the finite Schatten order required by a naive
$\Sp_4/\Sp_2$ Fredholm route.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $C_{\Gamma,R}^{-1}$ is compact and positive,
$C_{\Gamma,R}^{-1/2}=(C_{\Gamma,R}^{-1})^{1/2}$ is compact by continuous functional
calculus.  The operator $H_R$ is bounded at fixed $R$, hence
$K_R=C_{\Gamma,R}^{-1/2}H_R$ is compact; since $B_R$ is bounded,
$S_R=K_RB_RK_R^*$ is compact.

The symbol estimate~\eqref{eq:gamma-symbol} implies
\[
 c\log(2+|\xi|)\le m_\Gamma(\xi)\le C\log(2+|\xi|).
\tag{3.8}\label{eq:gamma-log-bounds}
\]
Choose a small interval $I\Subset(-R,R)$ and the localized orthonormal Fourier family
\[
 e_m(u)=|I|^{-1/2}1_I(u)e^{2\pi imu/|I|}.
\]
Then
\[
 \mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[e_m]\le C_I\log(2+m).
\tag{3.9}\label{eq:gamma-local-family}
\]
For a positive $C\ge I$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \langle C^{-1}f,f\rangle\,\langle Cf,f\rangle\ge\|f\|^4.
\]
Hence
\[
 \|C_{\Gamma,R}^{-1/2}e_m\|^2\ge\frac{c_I}{\log(2+m)}.
\tag{3.10}\label{eq:gamma-inverse-lower}
\]
The interval can be chosen so that one primitive $p=2$ translation copy in $H_R$ is
isolated from all other active translation copies.  Therefore
\[
 \|H_Re_m\|\ge c_2>0,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[H_Re_m]\le C_R\log(2+m),
\]
and again
\[
 \|K_Re_m\|^2\ge\frac{c_R}{\log(2+m)}.
\tag{3.11}\label{eq:K-lower}
\]
For $q\ge2$, membership $K_R\in\Sp_q$ would imply
\[
 \sum_m\|K_Re_m\|^q<\infty
\]
for every orthonormal family, contradicting~\eqref{eq:K-lower} because
$\sum_m(\log(2+m))^{-q/2}=\infty$.  Thus $K_R\notin\Sp_q$ for every finite $q\ge2$.
For $0<q<2$, the Schatten inclusion $\Sp_q\subset\Sp_2$ yields the same conclusion.
Finally, fixed-window boundedness of $R_R$ gives
\[
 B_R\ge\beta_RI,
 \qquad
 \beta_R=(1+\|R_R\|^2)^{-1}>0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 S_R=K_RB_RK_R^*\ge\beta_RK_RK_R^*.
\]
If $S_R\in\Sp_p$ for some finite $p\ge1$, eigenvalue monotonicity for positive compact
operators would imply $K_RK_R^*\in\Sp_p$, hence $K_R\in\Sp_{2p}$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:schatten}]\label{rem:schatten-scope}
This is a no-go for the \emph{specific} Gamma-preconditioned finite-window Schur
architecture.  It is not a no-go against a different global Object-X geometry or against
a future Fredholm realization by another mechanism.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Large-window Gamma limit}

On $L^2(\mathbb R)$ define
\[
 q_\Gamma(f)
 :=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}m_\Gamma(\xi)|\widehat f(\xi)|^2\,d\xi,
\tag{3.12}\label{eq:global-gamma-form}
\]
with positive selfadjoint operator $C_\Gamma$.
Identify the finite-window form domain with
\[
 \mathcal V_R:=\mathcal D(q_\Gamma)\cap
 \{f:\operatorname{supp}f\subset[-R,R]\}.
\]
Let
\[
 \Phi_R(f):=
 \begin{cases}
 q_\Gamma(f),&f\in\mathcal V_R,\\
 +\infty,&f\notin\mathcal V_R.
 \end{cases}
\tag{3.13}\label{eq:mosco-functional}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Mosco and strong-resolvent Gamma limit]\label{thm:mosco}
As $R\to\infty$,
\[
 \boxed{\Phi_R\xrightarrow[M]{}q_\Gamma.}
\tag{3.14}\label{eq:mosco-limit}
\]
Equivalently at the positive resolvent point $0$,
\[
 \boxed{
 E_RC_{\Gamma,R}^{-1}P_R
 \xrightarrow[s]{R\to\infty}
 C_\Gamma^{-1}
 }
\tag{3.15}\label{eq:strong-resolvent-gamma}
\]
on $L^2(\mathbb R)$.  This convergence is not norm-resolvent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The spaces $\mathcal V_R$ are increasing.  Their union is dense in
$\mathcal D(q_\Gamma)$ for the form norm: first approximate by Schwartz functions in the
weighted Fourier $L^2$ norm and then cut them off in physical space.  The logarithmic
weight satisfies
\[
 \log(2+|\xi|)\le C_\varepsilon(1+|\xi|^2)^\varepsilon,
\]
so the cutoffs converge in the Gamma form norm.  Weak lower semicontinuity of the closed
form gives the Mosco liminf condition, and the form-dense cutoffs give the recovery
sequence.

For $h\in L^2(\mathbb R)$, $C_\Gamma^{-1}h$ is the variational solution in the full form
domain and $E_RC_{\Gamma,R}^{-1}P_Rh$ is its $q_\Gamma$-orthogonal projection onto
$\mathcal V_R$; density yields~\eqref{eq:strong-resolvent-gamma}.  Each finite-window resolvent is compact,
whereas
\[
 C_\Gamma^{-1}\simeq M_{m_\Gamma^{-1}}
\]
is a nonzero multiplication operator on a nonatomic $L^2$ space and hence is not
compact.  A norm limit of compact operators would be compact, so norm-resolvent
convergence is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Gamma limit is not the Object-X limit]\label{rem:gamma-limit}
Theorem~\ref{thm:mosco} concerns the pure Gamma backbone.  It does not control the
large-$R$ limit of the full source-coupled Prime/Rest Schur geometry.  In particular,
the finite-window compactness is a confinement effect and cannot by itself supply the
global Object-X/Fredholm layer.
\end{remark}

\section{Graph transitions and normalized terminal transports}\label{sec:terminal}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded graph transitions]\label{thm:graph-transition}
For every $0<R<S$, zero extension maps the graph domain continuously into the larger
graph domain and satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 q_S^X(E_{R,S}f)
 \le(1+\|H_S\|^2)q_R^X(f).
 }
\tag{4.1}\label{eq:graph-transition-upper}
\]
Thus
\[
 \boxed{
 J_{R,S}:\KXR{R}\longrightarrow\KXR{S},
 \qquad J_{R,S}f=E_{R,S}f,
 }
\tag{4.2}\label{eq:graph-transition}
\]
is bounded and injective, and
\[
 \boxed{J_{S,T}J_{R,S}=J_{R,T}.}
\tag{4.3}\label{eq:transition-cocycle}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Gamma part is exactly invariant under zero extension:
\[
 \mathfrak c_{\Gamma,S}[E_{R,S}f]
 =\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[f].
\]
Furthermore
\[
 \langle\Sigma_SE_{R,S}f,E_{R,S}f\rangle
 \le\|H_S\|^2\|f\|_2^2
 \le\|H_S\|^2q_R^X(f).
\]
This proves~\eqref{eq:graph-transition-upper}.  Injectivity and the cocycle are inherited from zero extension.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-source lower bound]\label{cor:transition-lower}
For $0<R<T$,
\[
 \boxed{
 q_T^X(E_{R,T}f)
 \ge\frac{1}{1+\|H_R\|^2}\,q_R^X(f).
 }
\tag{4.4}\label{eq:transition-lower}
\]
Hence $J_{R,T}$ is bounded below on $\KXR{R}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Positivity of the Schur term and exact Gamma compatibility give
\[
 q_T^X(E_{R,T}f)
 \ge\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,T}[E_{R,T}f]
 =\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[f].
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:graph-norm-equiv} gives
\[
 q_R^X(f)\le(1+\|H_R\|^2)\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[f],
\]
which proves~\eqref{eq:transition-lower}.
\end{proof}

Define the positive terminal metric operator
\[
 \boxed{G_{R,T}:=J_{R,T}^*J_{R,T}\in\mathcal B(\KXR{R}).}
\tag{4.5}\label{eq:terminal-metric}
\]
By Corollary~\ref{cor:transition-lower},
\[
 G_{R,T}\ge(1+\|H_R\|^2)^{-1}I,
\]
so $G_{R,T}$ is boundedly invertible.

\begin{proposition}[Pullback identity]\label{prop:pullback}
For $0<R<S<T$,
\[
 \boxed{J_{R,S}^*G_{S,T}J_{R,S}=G_{R,T}.}
\tag{4.6}\label{eq:metric-pullback}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $J_{S,T}J_{R,S}=J_{R,T}$,
\[
 J_{R,S}^*G_{S,T}J_{R,S}=J_{R,T}^*J_{R,T}=G_{R,T}.
\]
\end{proof}

Define
\[
 \boxed{
 W_{R,S}^{[T]}
 :=G_{S,T}^{1/2}J_{R,S}G_{R,T}^{-1/2}.
 }
\tag{4.7}\label{eq:terminal-W}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite-terminal isometry and cocycle]\label{thm:terminal-isometry}
For every $0<R<S<T$,
\[
 \boxed{(W_{R,S}^{[T]})^*W_{R,S}^{[T]}=I.}
\tag{4.8}\label{eq:terminal-isometry}
\]
Whenever $0<R<S<U<T$,
\[
 \boxed{W_{S,U}^{[T]}W_{R,S}^{[T]}=W_{R,U}^{[T]}.}
\tag{4.9}\label{eq:terminal-cocycle}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:terminal-isometry} follows from~\eqref{eq:metric-pullback}; the cocycle follows by cancellation of
the middle factors and~\eqref{eq:transition-cocycle}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Finite isometry is not terminal convergence]\label{rem:notlimit}
Theorem~\ref{thm:terminal-isometry} is exact at fixed terminal horizon.  It does not
imply that $W_{R,S}^{[T]}$ is Cauchy as $T\to\infty$.
\end{remark}

\section{Relative future metrics}\label{sec:relative}

Fix $0<R<S<T<U$ and define
\[
 A_R^{T,U}:=G_{R,T}^{-1/2}G_{R,U}G_{R,T}^{-1/2},
\qquad
 A_S^{T,U}:=G_{S,T}^{-1/2}G_{S,U}G_{S,T}^{-1/2}.
\tag{5.1}\label{eq:relative-metrics}
\]
Write $W=W_{R,S}^{[T]}$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact relative compression]\label{thm:relative-compression}
\[
 \boxed{W^*A_S^{T,U}W=A_R^{T,U}.}
\tag{5.2}\label{eq:relative-compression}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert the definitions and use the pullback identity for terminal horizons $T$ and $U$.
\end{proof}

Define
\[
 Q:=\bigl(A_S^{T,U}\bigr)^{1/2}W\bigl(A_R^{T,U}\bigr)^{-1/2}.
\]

\begin{corollary}[Modulus isometry]\label{cor:modulus}
\[
 \boxed{Q^*Q=I.}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[First-power firewall]\label{rem:firstpower}
The exact identity $W^*A_SW=A_R$ does not imply invariance of $\Ran W$ under $A_S$, and
it does not determine the compression of $A_S^{1/2}$.  The missing information is
genuine square-root/range geometry.
\end{remark}

\section{Odd future asymptotics and conditioning}\label{sec:odd}

Let
\[
 f_-\in C_c^\infty((-R,R)),
 \qquad f_-(-u)=-f_-(u),
\]
and let
\[
 m:=\min\{j\ge0:\beta_R^{(j)}(f_-)\ne0\}
\]
be the first nonvanishing integral jet, whose existence follows from
Corollary~\ref{cor:first-jet}.  Put
\[
 c_m:=\binom{2m}{m}4^{-m}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Sharp fixed-vector odd future asymptotic]\label{thm:odd}
For fixed $R$ and fixed nonzero smooth odd $f_-$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_T(J_{R,T}f_-)
 =c_m^2|\beta_R^{(m)}(f_-)|^2
 \frac{e^T}{T^{2m+2}}(1+o(1)).
 }
\tag{6.1}\label{eq:odd-asymptotic}
\]
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Explicit rough complement and logarithmic gate}

Choose a nonzero smooth odd annulus vector $h\in C_c^\infty((-S,S))$ supported away from
$[-R,R]$ and define
\[
 \Pi^{\rm raw}
 :=J_{R,S}G_{R,T_0}^{-1}J_{R,S}^*G_{S,T_0},
\]
\[
 \boxed{g_h:=(I-\Pi^{\rm raw})h.}
\]
Then
\[
 g_h\in\mathcal C^-_{S,T_0}(R),
 \qquad g_h\ne0,
\]
and $g_h$ is compactly supported.  Its first nonzero integral jet has finite order
$m_h$.

For
\[
 \HG^\alpha(\mathbb R)
 :=\left\{F\in L^2:
 \int[\log(2+|\xi|)]^{2\alpha}|\widehat F(\xi)|^2d\xi<\infty
 \right\},
\]
one has
\[
 F\in\HG^\alpha
 \Longrightarrow
 \|\tau_tF-F\|_2
 =o\bigl((\log(1/t))^{-\alpha}\bigr)
 \qquad(t\downarrow0).
\]
The robust prime-cell quadrature closes for a compact odd vector with first integral jet
$m$ once
\[
 E_Sf\in\HG^{m+3/2}.
\]
The native Gamma form supplies only the baseline exponent $1/2$; the additional required
logarithmic order is $m+1$.

\begin{openproblem}[Logarithmic complement regularity]\label{open:log}
Does the explicit $g_h$ satisfy
\[
 E_Sg_h\in\HG^{m_h+3/2},
\]
or at least the corresponding direct translation-modulus condition on the prime-cell
scale?  A positive answer would produce a polynomial second-moment witness and rule out
the current Jensen-product sufficiency route.  It would still not rule out strong
terminal transport itself.
\end{openproblem}

\section{The open strong terminal transport problem}\label{sec:open}

The original source-level target is
\[
 \boxed{
 W_{R,S,-}^{[T]}
 \xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{\rm strong}?
 W_{R,S,-}^{[\infty]}.
 }
\]

Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth-odd-core} reduces strong Cauchy convergence to smooth compactly
supported odd vectors, and Theorem~\ref{thm:cross-terminal} identifies the exact
cross-terminal kernel.  Theorems~\ref{thm:odd} and~\ref{thm:mixed-jet} determine the
leading fixed-pair Schur geometry, but do not control the terminal square roots uniformly.

\begin{openproblem}[Strong odd terminal transport]\label{open:transport}
For fixed $0<R<S$, can one prove the strong Cauchy criterion
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle f,K_{R,S}^{T,U}f\rangle_{X,R}
 \longrightarrow\|f\|_{X,R}^2
\]
for every smooth compactly supported odd $f$ as $T,U\to\infty$?  In particular, can the
finite-jet Gram/square-root gate of Open Problem~\ref{open:finite-jet-sqrt} be resolved
without a fixed-vector-to-uniformity jump?
\end{openproblem}

\begin{remark}[Two research directions]\label{rem:routes}
The current theory leaves two distinct directions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the direct transport route through the exact cross-terminal Cauchy kernel and
        finite-jet square-root geometry;
  \item the O3 diagnostic route asking whether the sufficient Jensen-product mechanism
        is compatible with the superpolynomial odd conditioning.
\end{enumerate}
The second route is not a semantic replacement for the first.
\end{remark}

Residual observability, signed/clustered finite-band estimates and related quantities may
contribute to a direct route, but no equivalence between those diagnostics and strong
terminal transport is asserted here.

\section{Global obligations beyond the finite-horizon core}\label{sec:global}

Even a solution of Open Problem~\ref{open:transport} would not by itself complete the
full historical P11 scope.

\begin{openproblem}[Global nonorthogonal Gram/mediator closure]\label{open:gram}
Construct a canonical global positive geometry in which local prime-power channels and
the archimedean contribution arise from one nonorthogonal source/mediator mechanism,
rather than from an external additive juxtaposition.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Canonical source/adelic realization]\label{open:adelic}
Identify a global source space and canonical realization compatible with the finite
source-window geometry and the adelic/Weil amplitude port, including the correct
prime-power and archimedean normalization.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Global Fredholm/Schatten layer]\label{open:fredholm}
Determine whether the finite-horizon Feshbach operators admit a canonical global limit
with the Schatten/Fredholm control required for a genuine determinant or resolvent
formalism.  The finite-window Schatten obstruction of Theorem~\ref{thm:schatten} does
not decide this global question.
\end{openproblem}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The finite-horizon Object-X candidate geometry now has a source-explicit finite-adic
conditioning, a closed logarithmic Gamma graph space, positive Feshbach corrections,
canonical bounded graph transitions, exact terminal pullback metrics and normalized
transition isometries.  The finite-window Gamma/Feshbach geometry is compact but not of
finite Schatten order in the concrete B2-A sense; the pure Gamma backbone has a strong
large-window resolvent limit but loses finite-window compactness.

The absolute future metrics do not possess a bounded terminal limit.  Their odd growth
is organized by a complete pullback-compatible integral-jet hierarchy.  Nevertheless
the normalized relative transport has an exact cross-terminal Cauchy kernel.  Smooth odd
vectors form a dense graph core, and both diagonal and mixed fixed-pair Schur asymptotics
are explicit.  The leading normalized fixed-pair geometry becomes asymptotically rank
one, so the direct bottleneck has moved to subleading finite-jet Gram and square-root
control rather than to the existence of a mixed bilinear coefficient.

Strong odd terminal transport has still not been proved or disproved.  Separately,
superpolynomial odd conditioning shows that one natural Jensen-product sufficiency route
would require beyond-all-orders decay of a square-root compression defect.  The
second-moment/complement analysis reduces a possible obstruction to a logarithmic
regularity question, but resolving that diagnostic route would not by itself decide the
original strong transport problem.

Accordingly, the current status is
\[
 \boxed{
 \text{finite-horizon structural core: proved;}
 \quad
 \text{direct finite-jet square-root gate: open;}
 \quad
 \text{strong odd terminal transport: open;}
 \quad
 \text{P11-wide global closure: open.}
 }
\]
