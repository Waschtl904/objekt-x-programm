\begin{abstract}
P11 develops a finite-horizon Hilbert-space geometry for a source-first candidate of
Object~X and isolates, without overclaim, the remaining passage to a global terminal
geometry.  The construction combines a source-windowed archimedean Gamma form with a
finite-horizon Feshbach--Schur contribution and equips the resulting graph spaces with
positive terminal metric operators.  Their normalized transition maps are exact
isometries.  Relative future metrics satisfy an exact compression identity, and a second
normalization gives an isometric modulus map.  On the odd sector, the future Schur term
has a sharp fixed-vector asymptotic of size $e^T/T^{2m+2}$; at fixed base terminal this
forces superpolynomial growth of the associated odd condition number.  Consequently one
natural Jensen-product route to terminal convergence would require a square-root
compression defect that decays faster than every inverse power.

The central unresolved source-level problem remains
\[
 W_{R,S,-}^{[T]}\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{\rm strong}?W_{R,S,-}^{[\infty]}.
\]
P11 proves neither this limit nor its failure, does not construct a final global
Object~X, and contains no proof of the Riemann hypothesis.  The paper separates proved
finite-horizon structure, route-specific diagnostics, and open global obligations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, status semantics and non-claims}\label{sec:scope}

P11 organizes the positive finite-horizon geometry that survives the preceding no-go
analysis and states precisely what remains missing for a global Object-X construction.
The finite-window theory below must not be identified with the global limit problem.

\begin{remark}[Three distinct levels]\label{rem:levels}
We distinguish:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{finite-horizon structure}: exact Hilbert-space, Feshbach, metric and
        comparison identities at fixed windows;
  \item \emph{terminal transport}: asymptotic compatibility of normalized maps as the
        terminal horizon tends to infinity;
  \item \emph{P11-wide global geometry}: a canonical nonorthogonal global
        Gram/mediator realization, including eventual source/adelic and
        Fredholm/Schatten layers.
\end{enumerate}
A statement at one level is never silently promoted to the next.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Non-claims]\label{rem:nonclaims}
The present draft does not assert
\[
W_{R,S,-}^{[T]}\to W_{R,S,-}^{[\infty]}\quad\text{strongly},
\]
\[
\Theta_-\to0,
\qquad
\chi_-\|\Theta_-\|\to0,
\]
any polynomial lower witness for the actual P11 family, a global Fredholm determinant,
a final global Hilbert space $\KX$, or the Riemann hypothesis.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Draft provenance]\label{rem:provenance}
This first paper draft is distilled from the canonical P11 master ledger.  Historical

\end{remark}

\section{Fixed-window source geometry}\label{sec:source}

Fix $R>0$ and set
\[
 \mathscr H_R:=L^2(-R,R).
\]
For $0<R<S$, let
\[
 J_{R,S}:\mathscr H_R\longrightarrow\mathscr H_S
\]
be zero extension.  At a common terminal horizon $T>S$ we also write
$E_R=J_{R,T}$ and $E_S=J_{S,T}$.

The archimedean source form is encoded by
\[
 m_\Gamma(\xi):=1+g_\infty(\xi),
 \qquad
 m_\Gamma(\xi)\asymp\log(2+|\xi|).
\]
Define
\[
 \mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[f,g]
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 m_\Gamma(\xi)\widehat{E_Rf}(\xi)
 \overline{\widehat{E_Rg}(\xi)}\,d\xi.
\]
Let $C_{\Gamma,R}$ be the positive selfadjoint operator represented by this closed form.

\begin{proposition}[Gamma graph space]\label{prop:gamma}
For every fixed $R>0$, $\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}$ is densely defined, closed and positive.
Its graph completion defines the native source space
\[
 \KXR{R}:=\bigl(\mathcal D(\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}),\|\cdot\|_{X,R}\bigr).
\]
Moreover
\[
 C_{\Gamma,R}\ge I.
\]
The committed finite-window Gamma theorem gives compact embedding of this graph space
into $L^2(-R,R)$ and hence compact resolvent for the finite-window Gamma realization.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Logarithmic, not Sobolev, regularity]\label{rem:log}
Up to equivalent fixed-window norms, the native form threshold is
\[
 \mathcal D(\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R})
 \simeq
 \left\{f:\int\log(2+|\xi|)|\widehat{E_Rf}(\xi)|^2d\xi<\infty\right\}.
\]
It does not force the entire form domain into any fixed $H^s$, $s>0$.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite-horizon Feshbach geometry}\label{sec:feshbach}

At terminal horizon $T>R$, the source couples to a hub/rest system.  The exact
finite-horizon Schur contribution is
\[
 \Sigma_T
 =H_T(I+R_T^*R_T)^{-1}H_T^*\ge0.
\]
The source-restricted terminal form is
\[
 q_T^X(E_Rf,E_Rg)
 =\mathfrak c_{\Gamma,R}[f,g]
 +\langle\Sigma_R^{[T]}f,g\rangle_{L^2(-R,R)},
\]
where
\[
 \Sigma_R^{[T]}:=E_R^*\Sigma_T E_R
\]
is bounded, positive and selfadjoint.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded Schur perturbation]\label{prop:bounded-schur}
For fixed $R<T$,
\[
 F_R^{[T]}:=C_{\Gamma,R}+\Sigma_R^{[T]}
\]
is positive selfadjoint and
\[
 \mathcal D(F_R^{[T]})=\mathcal D(C_{\Gamma,R}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$(I+R_T^*R_T)^{-1}$ is a positive contraction and $H_T$ is bounded at fixed $T$;
hence $\Sigma_R^{[T]}$ is a bounded selfadjoint perturbation.  The standard bounded
perturbation theorem yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Full-rest martingale representation}

The rest Gram is not an orthogonal direct sum over prime powers $k$ inside a fixed prime
sector.  Its exact positive decomposition is indexed instead by prime/martingale-depth
pairs $(p,a)$.  With
\[
 \Phi_{p,a,T}[f](u)
 :=\sum_{k\ge a+1}p^{-3k/4}K_{k\log p}f(u),
\]
one has
\[
 \mathfrak R_T(f,g)
 =\sum_p(\log p)(p-1)\sum_{a\ge0}p^a
 \int_{\Omega_{p,a,T}}
 \Phi_{p,a,T}[f](u)\overline{\Phi_{p,a,T}[g](u)}\,du.
\]
Thus a canonical full analysis operator $\widetilde R_T$ satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\widetilde R_T^*\widetilde R_T=R_T^*R_T.}
\]

\begin{remark}[Primitive form-domination firewall]\label{rem:primitive}
We do not assume
\[
 R_T^*R_T\stackrel?\ge(R_T^{(1)})^*R_T^{(1)}.
\]
Different prime powers in one prime sector are nonorthogonal, so this form order is not
automatic.  The needed future-certificate transfer is instead obtained through the full
martingale analysis operator and the exponentially small higher-prime-power future tail.
\end{remark}

\section{Terminal metric operators and normalized transports}\label{sec:terminal}

For $R<T$, let
\[
 J_{R,T}:\KXR{R}\longrightarrow\KXR{T}
\]
be the terminal embedding and define
\[
 \boxed{G_{R,T}:=J_{R,T}^*J_{R,T}\in\mathcal B(\KXR{R}).}
\]
The operator $G_{R,T}$ is positive and boundedly invertible.

\begin{proposition}[Pullback identity]\label{prop:pullback}
For $0<R<S<T$,
\[
 \boxed{J_{R,S}^*G_{S,T}J_{R,S}=G_{R,T}.}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $J_{S,T}J_{R,S}=J_{R,T}$,
\[
 J_{R,S}^*G_{S,T}J_{R,S}
 =J_{R,T}^*J_{R,T}=G_{R,T}.
\]
\end{proof}

Define
\[
 \boxed{
 W_{R,S}^{[T]}
 :=G_{S,T}^{1/2}J_{R,S}G_{R,T}^{-1/2}.
 }
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite-terminal isometry and cocycle]\label{thm:terminal-isometry}
For every $0<R<S<T$,
\[
 \boxed{(W_{R,S}^{[T]})^*W_{R,S}^{[T]}=I.}
\]
Thus $W_{R,S}^{[T]}$ is an isometry.  More explicitly, whenever
\[
 0<R<S<U<T,
\]
the same-terminal maps satisfy
\[
 \boxed{W_{S,U}^{[T]}W_{R,S}^{[T]}=W_{R,U}^{[T]}.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The isometry follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:pullback}:
\[
\begin{aligned}
(W_{R,S}^{[T]})^*W_{R,S}^{[T]}
&=G_{R,T}^{-1/2}J_{R,S}^*G_{S,T}J_{R,S}G_{R,T}^{-1/2}\\
&=I.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the cocycle, the middle factors cancel and
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_{S,U}^{[T]}W_{R,S}^{[T]}
&=G_{U,T}^{1/2}J_{S,U}G_{S,T}^{-1/2}
  G_{S,T}^{1/2}J_{R,S}G_{R,T}^{-1/2}\\
&=G_{U,T}^{1/2}J_{R,U}G_{R,T}^{-1/2}
 =W_{R,U}^{[T]}.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Finite isometry is not terminal convergence]\label{rem:notlimit}
Theorem~\ref{thm:terminal-isometry} is exact at fixed terminal horizon.  It does not
imply that $W_{R,S}^{[T]}$ is Cauchy as $T\to\infty$.
\end{remark}

\section{Relative future metrics}\label{sec:relative}

Fix
\[
 0<R<S<T<U
\]
and define
\[
 A_R^{T,U}:=G_{R,T}^{-1/2}G_{R,U}G_{R,T}^{-1/2},
\]
\[
 A_S^{T,U}:=G_{S,T}^{-1/2}G_{S,U}G_{S,T}^{-1/2}.
\]
Write $W=W_{R,S}^{[T]}$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact relative compression]\label{thm:relative-compression}
\[
 \boxed{W^*A_S^{T,U}W=A_R^{T,U}.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert the definitions and use the pullback identity for $V=T$ and $V=U$.
\end{proof}

Define
\[
 Q:=\bigl(A_S^{T,U}\bigr)^{1/2}W\bigl(A_R^{T,U}\bigr)^{-1/2}.
\]

\begin{corollary}[Modulus isometry]\label{cor:modulus}
\[
 \boxed{Q^*Q=I.}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[First-power firewall]\label{rem:firstpower}
The exact identity
\[
 W^*A_SW=A_R
\]
does not imply invariance of $\Ran W$ under $A_S$, and it does not determine the
compression of $A_S^{1/2}$.  The missing information is genuine square-root/range
geometry.
\end{remark}

\section{Odd future asymptotics and conditioning}\label{sec:odd}

Let
\[
 f_-\in C_c^\infty((-R,R)),
 \qquad f_-(-u)=-f_-(u),
\]
and let
\[
 m:=\min\{j\ge0:\beta_R^{(j)}(f_-)\ne0\}
\]
be the first nonvanishing \emph{integral} jet.  These $\beta_R^{(j)}$ are not local
boundary derivatives.  Put
\[
 c_m:=\binom{2m}{m}4^{-m}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Sharp fixed-vector odd future asymptotic]\label{thm:odd}
For fixed $R$ and fixed nonzero smooth odd $f_-$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_T(J_{R,T}f_-)
 =c_m^2|\beta_R^{(m)}(f_-)|^2
 \frac{e^T}{T^{2m+2}}(1+o(1)).
 }
\]
The proof uses
\[
 \langle\mathbf1_T,(I+R_T^*R_T)\mathbf1_T\rangle=2T+O(1),
\]
a signed mean-zero future-edge certificate, prime-cell quadrature, and the full-rest
martingale lift of Section~\ref{sec:feshbach}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Draft 0.1 proof status]\label{rem:odd-proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:odd} is an audited result.  The first draft records its proof
architecture; the next proof pass must expand the constant-mode estimate, signed
certificate cost, prime quadrature and full-rest squeeze into self-contained lemmas.
\end{remark}

Fix a base terminal $T_0>R$.  The fixed-vector Rayleigh quotient satisfies
\[
 \rho_{T_0,U}(f_-)
 :=\frac{\langle G_{R,U}f_-,f_-\rangle}
         {\langle G_{R,T_0}f_-,f_-\rangle}
 \sim C_{T_0,f_-}\frac{e^U}{U^{2m+2}}.
\]
The audited odd comparison theorem then yields:

\begin{corollary}[Superpolynomial odd conditioning]\label{cor:conditioning}
For every $N>0$,
\[
 \boxed{
 U^{-N}\kappa(A_{T_0,U}^{R,-})\longrightarrow\infty.
 }
\]
Thus, with
\[
 \chi_{T_0,U}^{R,-}:=\kappa(A_{T_0,U}^{R,-})^{1/4},
\]
one has
\[
 \boxed{
 \forall N>0:\qquad U^{-N}\chi_{T_0,U}^{R,-}\longrightarrow\infty.
 }
\]
\end{corollary}

\section{Jensen geometry as a sufficient diagnostic route}\label{sec:jensen}

Fix $0<R<S<T_0<U$ and restrict to odd sectors.  Set
\[
 A_R:=A_{T_0,U}^{R,-},
 \qquad
 A_S:=A_{T_0,U}^{S,-},
 \qquad
 W:=W_{R,S,-}^{[T_0]}.
\]
Then
\[
 W^*A_SW=A_R,
 \qquad W^*W=I.
\]
Define
\[
 D:=W^*A_S^{1/2}W,
 \qquad
 \mathscr J:=A_R^{1/2}-D\ge0,
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta:=A_R^{-1/4}\mathscr J A_R^{-1/4},
 \qquad 0\le\Theta\le I,
 }
\]
and
\[
 \chi:=\|A_R^{1/4}\|\,\|A_R^{-1/4}\|=\kappa(A_R)^{1/4}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Relative Jensen Rayleigh formula]\label{prop:jensen}
\[
 \boxed{
 \|\Theta\|
 =\sup_{y\ne0}
 \left[
 1-
 \frac{\langle A_S^{1/2}Wy,Wy\rangle}
      {\langle A_R^{1/2}y,y\rangle}
 \right].
 }
\]
\end{proposition}

Let
\[
 P:=WW^*,
 \qquad
 Q:=A_S^{1/2}WA_R^{-1/2},
\]
\[
 \mathscr N:=(I-P)Q,
 \qquad
 \mathscr K:=I-W^*Q.
\]
Since $W$ is an isometry, $P$ is the orthogonal projection onto the closed range
$\Ran W$.  The exact defect balance is
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr K+\mathscr K^*
 =\mathscr K^*\mathscr K+\mathscr N^*\mathscr N.
 }
\]
Moreover
\[
 \mathscr K=A_R^{1/4}\Theta A_R^{-1/4},
\]
so
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac12\|\mathscr N\|^2
 \le\|\mathscr K\|
 \le\chi\|\Theta\|.
 }
\]

\begin{corollary}[Beyond-all-orders necessity for this route]\label{cor:beyond}
If the sufficient product mechanism
\[
 \chi_{T_0,U}^{R,-}\|\Theta_{T_0,U}^-\|\to0
\]
holds, then
\[
 \boxed{
 \forall N>0:\qquad U^N\|\Theta_{T_0,U}^-\|\to0.
 }
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Critical logical firewall]\label{rem:product}
This is a statement about one sufficient comparison mechanism.  The current theory does
not prove
\[
 \text{strong terminal transport}
 \Longrightarrow
 \chi\|\Theta\|\to0.
\]
Hence obstruction of the product mechanism would not prove nonconvergence of the strong
terminal transport.
\end{remark}

\section{Second-moment and complement diagnostics}\label{sec:diagnostic}

Define
\[
 \mathscr B:=(I-P)A_SW.
\]
Then
\[
 \mathscr B=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A_S\Ran W\subseteq\Ran W.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Second-moment compression variance]\label{prop:second}
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr B^*\mathscr B
 =W^*A_S^2W-A_R^2
 =:\Delta_2\ge0.
 }
\]
Furthermore
\[
 \boxed{
 \|\Theta\|
 \ge
 \frac{\|\Delta_2\|}{8\|A_R\|\|A_S\|}.
 }
\]
\end{proposition}

Put
\[
 \nu_2:=\frac{\|\Delta_2\|}{\|A_R\|\|A_S\|}.
\]
A polynomial lower bound for the actual P11 family would contradict the
beyond-all-orders requirement of Corollary~\ref{cor:beyond}.  Such a lower bound is not
proved here.

The exact future cross-Gram representation uses the fixed-$T_0$ complement
\[
 \mathcal C^-_{S,T_0}(R)
 :=\ker(J_{R,S}^*G_{S,T_0})\cap\KXR{S}^{-}.
\]
For $f\in\KXR{R}^{-}$ and $g\in\mathcal C^-_{S,T_0}(R)$, the norm of $\mathscr B$ is
controlled exactly by the future cross-Gram
\[
 \langle(G_{S,U}-G_{S,T_0})J_{R,S}f,g\rangle
\]
after the fixed metric normalizations.

\subsection{Explicit rough complement and logarithmic gate}

Choose a nonzero smooth odd annulus vector $h\in C_c^\infty((-S,S))$ supported away from
$[-R,R]$ and define
\[
 \Pi^{\rm raw}
 :=J_{R,S}G_{R,T_0}^{-1}J_{R,S}^*G_{S,T_0},
\]
\[
 \boxed{g_h:=(I-\Pi^{\rm raw})h.}
\]
Then
\[
 g_h\in\mathcal C^-_{S,T_0}(R),
 \qquad
 g_h\ne0,
\]
and $g_h$ is compactly supported.  Its first nonzero integral jet has finite order
$m_h$.

For
\[
 \HG^\alpha(\mathbb R)
 :=\left\{F\in L^2:
 \int[\log(2+|\xi|)]^{2\alpha}|\widehat F(\xi)|^2d\xi<\infty
 \right\},
\]
one has
\[
 F\in\HG^\alpha
 \Longrightarrow
 \|\tau_tF-F\|_2
 =o\bigl((\log(1/t))^{-\alpha}\bigr)
 \qquad(t\downarrow0).
\]
The robust prime-cell quadrature closes for a compact odd vector with first integral jet
$m$ once
\[
 E_Sf\in\HG^{m+3/2}.
\]
The native Gamma form supplies only the baseline exponent $1/2$; the additional required
logarithmic order is $m+1$.

\begin{openproblem}[Logarithmic complement regularity]\label{open:log}
Does the explicit $g_h$ satisfy
\[
 E_Sg_h\in\HG^{m_h+3/2},
\]
or at least the corresponding direct translation-modulus condition on the prime-cell
scale?  A positive answer would produce a polynomial second-moment witness and rule out
the current Jensen-product sufficiency route.  It would still not rule out strong
terminal transport itself.
\end{openproblem}

\subsection{Dirichlet--Riesz reduction}

Write
\[
 u_h:=G_{R,T_0}^{-1}J_{R,S}^*G_{S,T_0}h,
 \qquad
 g_h=h-J_{R,S}u_h.
\]
Then $J_{R,T_0}u_h$ is the $T_0$-metric orthogonal projection of $J_{S,T_0}h$ onto
$\Ran J_{R,T_0}$.  Equivalently,
\[
 q_{T_0}^X(E_Ru_h,E_Rv)
 =q_{T_0}^X(E_Sh,E_Rv)
 \qquad(v\in\KXR{R}).
\]
The forcing decomposes as
\[
 r_h=r_{\Gamma,h}+r_{\sigma,h}.
\]
For smooth interior $h$, the Gamma forcing has arbitrary finite logarithmic regularity;
the committed theory gives only $L^2$ control for the Schur forcing.  Consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 u_h
 =(C_{\Gamma,R}+\Sigma_R^{[T_0]})^{-1}r_h
 \in\mathcal D(C_{\Gamma,R}).
 }
\]
No result here upgrades this operator-domain gain to the threshold in
Open Problem~\ref{open:log}.

\begin{remark}[Dirichlet Fourier firewall]\label{rem:dirichlet}
The metric inverse $G_{R,T_0}^{-1}$ is not the global Fourier multiplier
$(1+g_\infty)^{-1}$.  The finite-window source compression is a genuine Dirichlet/Riesz
problem and cannot be replaced by global symbol inversion.
\end{remark}

\section{The open strong terminal transport problem}\label{sec:open}

The original source-level target is
\[
 \boxed{
 W_{R,S,-}^{[T]}
 \xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{\rm strong}?
 W_{R,S,-}^{[\infty]}.
 }
\]

\begin{openproblem}[Strong odd terminal transport]\label{open:transport}
For fixed $0<R<S$, is $W_{R,S,-}^{[T]}$ strongly Cauchy as the common terminal horizon
$T\to\infty$?  Can the exact source-level cross-terminal Cauchy kernel be controlled on
the full odd graph space?
\end{openproblem}

\begin{remark}[Two research directions]\label{rem:routes}
The current theory leaves two distinct directions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a direct transport route attacking the cross-terminal Cauchy asymptotic itself;
  \item the O3 diagnostic route asking whether the sufficient Jensen-product mechanism
        is compatible with the superpolynomial odd conditioning.
\end{enumerate}
The second route is not a semantic replacement for the first.
\end{remark}

Residual observability, signed/clustered finite-band estimates and related quantities may
contribute to a direct route, but no equivalence between those diagnostics and strong
terminal transport is asserted here.

\section{Global obligations beyond the finite-horizon core}\label{sec:global}

Even a solution of Open Problem~\ref{open:transport} would not by itself complete the
full historical P11 scope.

\begin{openproblem}[Global nonorthogonal Gram/mediator closure]\label{open:gram}
Construct a canonical global positive geometry in which local prime-power channels and
the archimedean contribution arise from one nonorthogonal source/mediator mechanism,
rather than from an external additive juxtaposition.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Canonical source/adelic realization]\label{open:adelic}
Identify a global source space and canonical realization compatible with the finite
source-window geometry and the adelic/Weil amplitude port, including the correct
prime-power and archimedean normalization.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Global Fredholm/Schatten layer]\label{open:fredholm}
Determine whether the finite-horizon Feshbach operators admit a canonical global limit
with the Schatten/Fredholm control required for a genuine determinant or resolvent
formalism.  Finite Feshbach identities alone do not provide such a limit.
\end{openproblem}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The finite-horizon Object-X candidate geometry provides a closed logarithmic Gamma graph
space, positive Feshbach corrections, exact terminal pullback metrics, normalized
transition isometries, relative future compression, a modulus isometry, and sharp odd
fixed-vector future asymptotics.  These results survive the preceding no-go analysis and
form a coherent source-first finite-horizon structure.

The main asymptotic bottleneck is nevertheless still open.  Strong odd terminal
transport has not been proved or disproved.  Superpolynomial odd conditioning shows that
one natural Jensen-product sufficiency route would require beyond-all-orders decay of a
square-root compression defect.  The second-moment/complement analysis reduces a
possible obstruction to a concrete logarithmic regularity question, but resolving that
diagnostic route would still not by itself decide the original strong transport problem.

Accordingly, the current status is
\[
 \boxed{
 \text{finite-horizon structural core: proved;}
 \quad
 \text{strong odd terminal transport: open;}
 \quad
 \text{P11-wide global closure: open.}
 }
\]
