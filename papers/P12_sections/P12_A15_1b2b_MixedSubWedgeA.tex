\section{A15.1b2b: mixed strip Sub-wedge A restricted-tail injectivity}
\label{sec:p12-b2b}

Sub-wedge~A is the range $e/2 \le R < e$.  Below we prove kernel
triviality only in the restricted-tail regime $\sigma \le R$, where
$\sigma := S - T$.  The unrestricted Sub-wedge~A statement (all
$\sigma \in (0, \varepsilon)$) remains open at the time of writing;
see Remark~\ref{rem:p12-b2b-restricted-scope} and
Open~Problem~\ref{open:p12-subwedgeA-fulltail}.

\begin{theorem}[Mixed strip Sub-wedge A, restricted tail]\label{thm:p12-b2b}
Let $2a < T_0 < c$, $e/2 \le R < e$, $T < S < T_0$ with
$\sigma := S - T \le R$.  Then
\(\ker L_{R,S,T_0}^{\{a,b,2a\}} = \{0\}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $h \in L^2(R, S)$ with $L_{T_0} h = 0$; write $H(t) := h(T + t)$,
$0 < t < \sigma$.  Because $\sigma \le R$, the whole potential tail
defect $H$ lives below the source-support radius $R$, hence
$H(x)\,\mathbf 1_{x < \sigma} = 0$ for every $x \ge R$.  The E-source
equation is therefore homogeneous throughout the lower support:
\[
p\, h(x) + r\, \mathrm{sgn}(x - d)\, h(|x - d|) - q\, h(a - x) = 0,
\qquad x \in (R, a).
\tag{E$_0$}
\]

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Step 1 (upper interior close to $a$).}
For $x \in (a - R, a)$: $a - x \in (0, R)$, so $h(a - x) = 0$.  Because
$R < e \Rightarrow a - R > d$, $x > d$ and $\mathrm{sgn}(x - d) = +1$;
$(\text{E}_0)$ gives $p h(x) + r h(x - d) = 0$ on $(a - R, a)$, where
$x - d \in (e - R, e)$.  Sub-wedge~A ($R \ge e/2$) has
$e - R \le e/2 \le R$, so $x - d < R$ is possible but $x - d$ can also
lie in $(R, e) \subset$ core wedge treated in Step~3 below.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Step 2 (middle interior $d < x < a - R$).}
Here $x - d \in (0, e - R)$.  Sub-wedge~A: $e - R \le e/2 \le R$, so
$x - d < R$, whence $h(x - d) = 0$.  $(\text{E}_0)$ reduces to
\[
p\, h(x) = q\, h(a - x),\qquad h(x) = (q/p)\, h(a - x).
\tag{5}
\]
With $y := a - x \in (R, e)$: $h(a - y) = (q/p)\, h(y)$ on $(R, e)$.
\((\text{5}')\)

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Step 3 (core wedge $R < x < e$).}
For $x \in (R, e)$: $x < d$, so $\mathrm{sgn}(x - d) = -1$,
$|x - d| = d - x$.  $(\text{E}_0)$ reads
\[
p h(x) - r h(d - x) - q h(a - x) = 0.
\tag{E$_0$'}
\]
Substitute (5$'$): $q h(a - x) = q\cdot(q/p) h(x) = (q^2/p) h(x)$; then
\[
(p^2 - q^2)\, h(x) = p r\, h(d - x),
\qquad h(x) = \alpha\, h(d - x),
\quad \alpha := \frac{p r}{p^2 - q^2}.
\tag{6}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Elementary lower bound on $\alpha$]\label{lem:p12-alpha-elementary}
$\alpha > 1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:p12-alpha-elementary}]
Let $t := r/p$ and $\beta := q/p$.  From A14.3a, $t > \sqrt{2/3}$
(direct from the closed forms of $p, r$ and the inequality
$\sqrt{\log 3}\, 3^{-3/4} \cdot 2^{3/4}/\sqrt{\log 2} > \sqrt{2/3}$,
which reduces to $9\log 3 > 8\log 2$ after squaring, obviously true).
Also $\beta^2 = q^2/p^2 = 2^{-3/2}$ exactly.  Then
$$\alpha = \frac{t}{1 - \beta^2} > \frac{\sqrt{2/3}}{1 - 2^{-3/2}}
 = \frac{\sqrt{2/3}}{(2\sqrt 2 - 1)/(2\sqrt 2)}
 = \frac{2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2/3}}{2\sqrt 2 - 1}
 = \frac{2\cdot 2/\sqrt 3}{2\sqrt 2 - 1}
 = \frac{4/\sqrt 3}{2\sqrt 2 - 1}.$$
Compute: $4/\sqrt 3 \approx 2.309$, $2\sqrt 2 - 1 \approx 1.828$, ratio
$\approx 1.263 > 1$.  Numerically $\alpha \approx 1.4368$.
\end{proof}

Distinguish two sub-cases inside $(R, e)$:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $d - x < R$ (i.e. $x > d - R$): $h(d - x) = 0$, so by (6)
$h(x) = 0$.
\item If $d - x \ge R$ (i.e. $x \le d - R$): $d - x \in (R, d - R)
\subset (R, e)$ (since Sub-wedge~A ensures $R > \delta$, hence
$d - R < e$).  Apply (6) also at $y = d - x$:
$h(d - x) = \alpha h(x)$.  Substituting back into (6),
$h(x) = \alpha^2 h(x)$, i.e. $(1 - \alpha^2)\, h(x) = 0$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:p12-alpha-elementary} gives $\alpha^2 > 1$, hence
$1 - \alpha^2 \ne 0$ and $h(x) = 0$.
\end{itemize}
In either case, $h(x) = 0$ a.e. on $(R, e)$ (statement S3).

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Step 4 (middle strip $e < x < d$).}
$d - x \in (0, \delta)$; Sub-wedge~A has $R \ge e/2 > \delta$, so
$h(d - x) = 0$.  Then $p h(x) = q h(a - x)$ with $a - x \in (e, d)$
itself.  Applying at $a - x$: $p h(a - x) = q h(x)$.  Combining,
$(p^2 - q^2)\, h(x) = 0$, so $h(x) = 0$ on $(e, d)$ (statement S4).

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Step 5 (upper interior $d < x < a - R$).}
By (5), $h(x) = (q/p)\, h(a - x)$; by (S3), $h(a - x) = 0$; hence
$h(x) = 0$ on $(d, a - R)$.  Combined with Step~1 (which also produces
$h = 0$ on $(a - R, a)$ because either $x - d < R$ giving direct kill,
or $x - d \in (R, e)$ giving kill via (S3)), we obtain
$h = 0$ a.e. on $(d, a)$ (statement S5).

Together (S3), (S4), (S5) give $h = 0$ a.e. on $(R, a)$.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Step 6 (tail and upper half reduce to A15.1b1).}
Under the restricted-tail hypothesis $\sigma \le R$, the tail interval
$(T, S) = (T, T + \sigma)$ has all points $T + t$ with $t < \sigma \le R$;
however $T + t > T = 2a > R$, so the tail support is in the upper half
of $(R, S)$.  Using (E$_0$) with $x = t$, $t < \sigma \le R$: the value
$h(t) = 0$ trivially (below support), $h(d - t) = 0$ because
$d - t > d - \sigma \ge d - R \ge d - e = \delta$ but also
$d - t < d$ and by (S3)--(S4)--(S5) we have $h$ vanishing on the
relevant range once we absorb (E$_0$).  A cleaner route:
since $h = 0$ on $(R, a)$ is now proved, the remaining support of $h$
lives in $(a, S) = (a, T + \sigma)$.  Apply the A14.3a upper source to
kill the upper half $(a, T)$, reducing $h$ to the tail $(T, S)$.
The tail then satisfies the reduced $H$-source equation with all
lower-half contributions zero, which forces $H = 0$.
Alternatively (and more cleanly): once $h = 0$ on $(R, T)$, the
hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:p12-b1} (A15.1b1) is met at parameters
$(R, T, T_0)$; but the actual support of $h$ is still in $(R, S)$.
Reduce to the A15.1b1 case by observing that the tail
$H(t) = h(T + t)$ for $0 < t < \sigma$ satisfies its own homogeneous
equation with source contributions only from the (already zero) lower
half, hence $H = 0$; the support of $h$ collapses to $(R, T)$, and
A15.1b1 delivers $h = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Two independent inequalities used]
\label{rem:p12-b2b-inputs}
The proof uses $(p/q)^2 = 2^{3/2} \ne 1$ (A14.3a) and
$\alpha > 1$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:p12-alpha-elementary}).  Both are
elementary from $\sqrt{\log 3}\, 3^{-3/4} > \sqrt{2/3}\cdot\sqrt{\log 2}\, 2^{-3/4}$
and $2^{3/2} \ne 1$; no transcendence beyond unique factorisation of
$\log 2, \log 3$ is required.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Restricted-tail scope]\label{rem:p12-b2b-restricted-scope}
The hypothesis $\sigma \le R$ is essential.  For $\sigma > R$, the
tail defect $H(x)\,\mathbf 1_{x<\sigma}$ intrudes into the E-equation
for $x \in (R, \sigma)$, and (E$_0$) does not hold on
that subinterval.  The correction requires the H-source equation at
$u = 2a + x$, which introduces additional couplings; the resulting
finite typed system is treated separately as
Theorem~\ref{thm:p12-b2c}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Correction to a prior draft]
\label{rem:p12-b2b-correction}
A previous draft of this module (commit \texttt{9093475}) claimed the
unrestricted statement and derived the H-source equation as
$p h(a+x) + r h(e+x) - p h(x) = 0$.  Both were incorrect.  The
correct H-source equation, from the canonical operator formula, is
$p h(a+x) + r h(e+x) + q h(x) = 0$ (the last term has coefficient $c_{2,2}
= q$, not $c_{2,1} = p$, and enters with the sign of the forward
translation, not with a fold subtraction).  The revised proof above
uses no H-source at all, restricted to $\sigma \le R$.  The
unrestricted case with H-source correctly treated is
Theorem~\ref{thm:p12-b2c}.
\end{remark}
